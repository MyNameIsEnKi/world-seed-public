# World Seed — Core Vision

**Status:** Public core vision  
**Creator / Vision Holder:** Adonté Gary Bossé

World Seed is intended to be larger than any one person, discipline, studio, or generation of technology.

The project begins with a vision held and developed by **Adonté Gary Bossé**, but it is not meant to become a system in which one person must understand, design, program, test, create, research, and maintain everything alone. The scale of World Seed makes that neither desirable nor necessary.

## The Collective Construction Rule

> **World Seed should be designed so that no single person needs to understand or build the entire system alone. Every major idea must be decomposable into clearly owned parts with stable interfaces, documented assumptions, provenance, tests, and integration rules, allowing many people and AI systems to contribute without losing the original vision.**

World Seed should be something people build **with** one another.

That includes developers, artists, writers, musicians, researchers, scientists, engineers, designers, performers, worldbuilders, security specialists, accessibility specialists, testers, legal and business experts, community members, players, and future disciplines that may not yet exist.

AI can assist that work, but AI is not meant to erase human authorship or replace the people who bring knowledge, experience, judgment, creativity, culture, and care to the project.

## The Role of the Vision Holder

Adonté does not need to be the best programmer, scientist, artist, engineer, writer, or researcher in every room.

His role is to remain an active participant while holding and communicating the larger vision, asking difficult questions, protecting the project's intent, connecting ideas across systems, learning from specialists, making foundational decisions when required, and helping create an architecture in which other people can do excellent work without having to reconstruct the entire vision from scratch.

World Seed should make room for contributors to challenge ideas, expose weaknesses, improve systems, discover better approaches, and create things that the original creator could not have created alone.

Success is not everyone thinking like Adonté.

Success is building something strong enough that other people can understand it, question it, improve it, extend it, and eventually take parts of it farther than he could.

## From Vision to Buildable Reality

World Seed should not stop at ambitious concepts. Major ideas should eventually be translated through a clear engineering path:

**Vision → Architecture → System → Subsystem → Component → Interface → Implementation → Test**

For every significant system, future specifications should make it possible for a development team to answer concrete questions such as:

- What exactly is this system supposed to accomplish?
- What are its inputs and outputs?
- What other systems does it depend on?
- What hardware, software, data, models, sensors, or infrastructure does it require?
- Who or what is allowed to change its state?
- How is authorship and provenance preserved?
- What expertise is required to build and maintain it?
- How does another team integrate with it without needing to understand the entire project?
- How do we test whether it works?
- What happens when it fails?
- Which parts can be built with current technology?
- Which parts require substantial engineering?
- Which parts remain experimental, research-dependent, or speculative?

A futuristic idea does not need to be discarded simply because every part of it cannot be built today. Instead, World Seed should define the smallest stable interfaces and acceptance conditions the future capability must satisfy. Current implementations can then sit behind those interfaces until better technology becomes available.

## Feasibility Must Be Honest

World Seed should distinguish aspiration from implementation.

A proposed capability may be classified as:

- **BUILDABLE NOW** — existing technology is sufficient.
- **BUILDABLE WITH ENGINEERING** — substantial implementation work is required, but no fundamental breakthrough is assumed.
- **EXPERIMENTAL** — technically plausible, but reliability, cost, scale, or usability is not yet sufficient.
- **RESEARCH DEPENDENCY** — important capabilities depend on immature research or technology.
- **SPECULATIVE** — preserved intentionally as a future target without pretending that a present implementation exists.

The goal is that one day Adonté can place a World Seed specification in front of a team of specialists and ask:

> **“Can we build this?”**

And the team can give a meaningful answer — yes, no, not yet, or yes with clearly identified constraints — because the idea has been specified deeply enough to evaluate.

## Learning Is Part of the Foundation

World Seed does not present Adonté as omniscient.

The project exists partly because he has learned from other people, from many disciplines, from disagreement, from mistakes, from art, science, philosophy, technology, culture, nature, experience, and questions that remain unanswered.

Knowledge inside World Seed should retain that humility. Sources, contributors, influences, decisions, uncertainty, revisions, and provenance should be preserved wherever practical.

No mind begins from zero. Every contributor inherits knowledge from others, transforms it through their own work, and can leave something useful for whoever comes next.

## Build With Everyone

World Seed should never require its creator to become every specialist necessary to realize it.

The project should instead become a place where many people can eventually say:

> **“I helped build that.”**

Adonté remains part of the project and part of its foundation, but the goal is not dependency on Adonté.

The goal is a living architecture capable of supporting collaboration at enormous scale while preserving authorship, consent, responsibility, provenance, interoperability, and the ability to evolve.

If World Seed becomes what it is intended to become, its greatest achievements will not belong to one person alone.

They will have been built together.
